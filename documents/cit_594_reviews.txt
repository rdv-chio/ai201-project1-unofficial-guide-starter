The course is certainly no longer the super easy automatic A that is described in reviews from mid 2022 and earlier. The assignments are tough, take time, and often leave you scratching your head due to how poorly they might align with the current week’s material. While there is extra credit, I wouldn’t recommend attempting them unless they cover a topic you’ve learned from another course or in your employment. They cover topics not taught in the class, take a decent amount of time, and have zero sum grading policy surrounding them. I spent 8 hours on 2 extra credit assignments and got no credit on each. The best strategy in the course is probably to focus on getting near perfect grades on the assignments. This is because there is a very good chance you’re going to fail the final exam. The class average on the final was roughly 60% and there was no curve that I was aware of. There are 3 reasons for this low score. First, the weekly assignments aren’t timed but the exam is. This makes a big difference. I simply ran out of time on the programming portion of the exam and got a zero on this. The second reason is the unconventional grading method for the multiple choice portion of the exam which allows for incorrect answers to offset points gained from correct answers. Lastly, the exam tended to focus more on the topics in the course for which you hadn’t previously been in some way assessed during the semester. For example, if you covered a topic in a homework assignment, meaning you got the opportunity to really reinforce it and learn it well, it seemed less likely to show up in the exam. To me this runs counter to how you might construct an exam since you might expect areas of greater focus in the course to be the important topics on the final assessment. This being said, I failed the exam, which was 15% of the total grade, and still got an A in the course due to stellar assignment grades. Exam aside grade aside, I truly feel like I learned a lot in this course, have a far better understanding of data structures and their use, and very much had a positive learning experience in the final project. I think most students feel this way in spite of the final assessment not reflecting this. There is still some room for improvement, which seems to be the ongoing trend for this course over the past couple of years.

I'll share additional context in case it's useful. I'm a turtle and before 594 I took 591 and 592. I received an A+ in 591, an A in 592, and an A- in 594.

In 594, there are 4 major components to grading: quizzes (~15%), assignments (~45%), coding projects (25%), and the final exam (15%). There is also an extra credit assignment worth ~5% of your final grade. I believe an A is reasonably manageable to achieve in this class, but I'll outline why I received an A-, some learnings, and some comparisons to 591/592.

Firstly, the quizzes are basically free points. The class is very generous in weighting them so heavily.

The assignments are overall more difficult than 591. They are also entirely autograded. I encourage you to spend as much time thinking about edge cases and testing your program as you do actually writing your program. The workload for this class is otherwise deceptively small. If you're doing adequate testing then you'll spend a lot more time per week on the class. I averaged an A on the assignments, though there were a couple times I was lazy about testing, which I regret. On only one assignment (graphs) did I genuinely feel stumped and ended up submitting knowing that there were some edge cases it wouldn't pass. I however did better on this assignment than some easier assignments where I was lazy about testing. So test!

There is a solo and a group project back to back. They are a significant amount of time. They are both autograded and manually graded. The manual grading is for the design of your program. I received a B on the solo project and an A on the group project. My solo project received 100% for functionality, but the grader disliked some of my design decisions. While I made some changes for the group project, ultimately I think there's a fair amount of luck involved in getting a kind grader.

The final exam was, in my opinion, quite difficult. I scored the exact median grade. There was a small curve but not enough to really make much of a difference to my grade. I think the final is written to be needlessly tricky / confusing.

My biggest regret, though, was skipping the extra credit assignment. It is not a small amount of work but it is useful for the group project. Had I not skipped it I would have gotten an A in the class.

Compared to 592, I thought this class was both easier in terms of concepts and workload, but if you slack off it is easy for your grade to tank. I think this class has a reputation for being "easy" but I would take the class with the mindset that it is regular difficulty, just maybe easier compared to some other classes in the program.

I wish the course was set up to allow you to have at least one more submit, if not unlimited submits, to promote learning and figuring out where your code went wrong without penalizing students for minor fixable errors.
